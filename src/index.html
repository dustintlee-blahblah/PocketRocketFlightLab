    About Pocket Rocket Flight Lab

    · by Dustin Lee

    © 2026 Dustin Lee · Free software under the GNU General Public License, version 3 or later

    Full 6-DOF flight simulation (X, Y, Z, pitch, yaw, roll) based on published aerospace industry and physics literature.

    Treat all results as estimates and confirm with your RSO.

    Built-in motor curves: the OpenRocket motor database (from ThrustCurve.org).

    Aerodynamic methods

    Drag is built up component by component from published methods:

    • Hoerner, S. F., Fluid-Dynamic Drag, 1965: body and fin form factors, forebody shoulder drag, boattail separation, protuberance drag.
    • Schlichting, H., Boundary-Layer Theory: turbulent flat-plate friction and admissible roughness.
    • White, F. M., Viscous Fluid Flow: fully rough flat-plate friction.
    • Nikuradse, J., 1933: smooth and fully rough roughness regimes.
    • Stoney, W. E., Collection of Zero-Lift Drag Data on Bodies of Revolution from Free-Flight Investigations, NASA TR R-100, 1961: measured transonic and supersonic nose drag by shape and fineness.
    • Fleeman, E. L., Tactical Missile Design, AIAA: base drag (coasting and powered) and supersonic drag of body flares, after Bonney, Engineering Supersonic Aerodynamics, 1950.
    • Lees, L., 1955: modified Newtonian pressure on rounded fin edges and rail buttons.
    • Ackeret, J., 1925: supersonic thin-airfoil wave drag.
    • Allen, H. J. and Perkins, E. W., NACA Report 1048, 1951: body crossflow force at angle of attack and crossflow drag for tumbling descent.
    • Mandell, G. K., Caporaso, G. J. and Bengen, W. P., Topics in Advanced Model Rocketry, 1973: pitch motion, aerodynamic damping and jet damping.
    • Barrowman, J. S., The Practical Calculation of the Aerodynamic Characteristics of Slender Finned Vehicles, 1967: center of pressure.
    • Galejs, R., Wind instability: what Barrowman left out, 1999: body lift at angle of attack.
    • NASA TN D-4013 and TN D-4014, 1967: measured center of pressure of the Arcas sounding rocket, Mach 0.6–4.6.
    • Crowell, G. A., The Descriptive Geometry of Nose Cones, 1996: nose-cone shapes.
    • Sutherland's law for air viscosity; U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976.

    Design files and altimeter files

    Opens OpenRocket (.ork) and RockSim (.rkt) design files and saves designs as OpenRocket (.ork) files; RockSim files are only read, never changed. It also reads flight data files exported from altimeters, so they can be used alongside those programs and devices. File support was written independently from the files themselves; no part of OpenRocket, RockSim or any altimeter software is included in this app. Simulations this app saves into a design file are labeled “Pocket Rocket”, so they aren’t mistaken for that program’s own results.

    Motor data

    Built-in AeroTech, Cesaroni, Loki Research and Estes thrust curves come from the OpenRocket motor database, which collects curves published on ThrustCurve.org, most of them from official motor certification tests. Where a motor has more than one curve, the certification curve is used. Other motors can be loaded from .eng, .rse or .zip files. thrustcurve.org
